# The design process is dead. Here’s what’s replacing it.

> Canonical source: https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/the-design-process-is-dead
> Original interview / podcast, 2026-03-01.

## Source locator

Jenny Wen, then leading design for Claude / Claude Cowork at Anthropic, describes several changes in the design role:

- the classic discovery → mock → iterate process no longer maps cleanly to the speed of AI-assisted engineering;
- in her retrospective estimate, mocking/prototyping moved from roughly 60–70% of design work to roughly 30–40%;
- designers increasingly help engineering teams execute instead of only delivering a finished design artifact;
- multi-year design visions are compressed toward roughly 3–6 month horizons and may be represented by directional prototypes rather than polished long-range artifacts;
- AI may improve at taste/judgment, so designers should not assume those capabilities remain uniquely human forever;
- despite that, someone still has to decide what gets built, what matters and who is accountable for the decision;
- her hiring model is changing toward multiple designer archetypes rather than one uniform craft profile.

## Evidence boundary

This is a first-person leadership interview, not a time-study or controlled productivity analysis. The 60–70% → 30–40% figures are experiential estimates. "The design process is dead" is a deliberately strong framing about workflow compression, not evidence that research, user understanding or iteration literally disappear. The accountability statement supports human decision ownership in the current organization, not a timeless human-only capability theorem.
