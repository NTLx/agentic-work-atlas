# A New Look at AI's Impact on Jobs

> Canonical paper: https://ssrn.com/abstract=7024799
> Date written: 2026-06-30.

## Source locator

- Authors: Ara Kharazian (Ramp), Lisa Simon (Revelio Labs), Ryan Stevens (Ramp).
- Links observed AI spending from Ramp corporate card / bill-pay data to Revelio Labs workforce records.
- Sample: 21,559 U.S. firms.
- Firms are classified by AI spending per employee in the first three months after adoption.
- Reported findings:
  - high-intensity adopters grow employment by roughly 10% following adoption;
  - low-intensity adopters show no statistically significant employment change;
  - entry-level headcount rises about 12% for high-intensity adopters;
  - gains are broad across roles including engineering, sales, administration and customer service.
- The paper explicitly notes adopter selection:
  - adopters are larger;
  - more technical;
  - faster-growing before/around adoption;
  - sector gains are concentrated in Information.

## Evidence boundary

This is an observational linked-data study, not randomized adoption. AI spending observed through Ramp is a platform-selected adoption proxy and may miss AI acquired through other channels. Entry-level headcount is a stock measure, not hires, promotion, training completion or independent capability. The study therefore directly rejects a simple claim that high-intensity AI adoption must immediately shrink entry-level headcount, but it does not establish that professional skill reproduction remains intact.
