# Agentic AI and Human-in-the-Loop Interventions

> Canonical source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.14830
> Experiment period: 2024; paper released 2026.

## Source locator

- Randomized field experiment in Alibaba Taobao customer service operations.
- 647 customer-service workers and 680,676 service chats.
- Treatment workers supervise an agentic AI system for AI-eligible chats while continuing to handle AI-ineligible chats; control workers handle all chats manually.
- AI deployment reduces average chat duration but substantially lowers ratings for AI-eligible chats.
- Human intervention is more effective for algorithm-triggered technical escalations than emotional escalations.
- Emotional escalations show lower post-escalation human effort: fewer messages, smaller share of conversation rounds, less proactive information search and solution provision.
- Earlier human intervention is associated with stronger post-escalation effort and better service preservation.
- Positive spillover is reported for AI-ineligible chats as workers reallocate attention.

## Evidence boundary

Randomization identifies the effect of AI deployment at the worker level, not the causal effect of escalation type, packet content or intervention timing. The timing/type analyses are mechanism/heterogeneity analyses within the deployed condition and should not be described as independently randomized treatments.
